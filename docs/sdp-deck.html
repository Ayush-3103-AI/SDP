xqi

Explainable, safe correction for FFF 3D printing.

Ayush Kabbur · K Tharun · Krish Sharma · Sanjana Dhongadi
Guides: Dr. Sharada K. Shiragudikar · Dr. Vinod Kumar V. Meti

One-line story: our seniors' printer already detects defects and fixes them with fixed recipes. We add the missing half: which setting is likely off, how sure the model is, what to change, and a safety gate that bounds any change before a human approves it. xqi = explainable quality intelligence, CARR topic 4.1.3. Everything measured is offline on the public CAXTON data and a simulated printer.
01 The problem

A failing print is visible. Its cause is not.

Warping

Layer shift

Mostly mechanical. Speed?

Under-extrusion

Flow, speed, heat, Z?

Stringing

Nozzle too hot?

Possible contributors from FFF practice, not findings of this study.

FFF is the most common desktop additive process and one of the least forgiving: a bad flow rate, speed, nozzle temperature or nozzle height ruins a print within a few layers. Each visible defect has several possible process causes (paper Table I). A camera can see the defect; it cannot directly see which setting produced it. That gap is the project.
01 The problem

A detector answers what. Operators need why.

Why

Which setting is off?

Flow, speed, heat or Z.

What

What should change?

The smallest undoing step.

Safe?

Can we change it now?

Mid-print, from a model that may be wrong.

A detector that only names the defect leaves the operator's next three questions open. And a system that changes printer settings on its own from a model that may be wrong adds a failure mode of its own. So the third question, safety, has to be answered independently of model quality.

The CARR topic brief audits the current loop against the topic title. Quality intelligence: present, but severity is binary. Corrective: present, but recipes are fixed, not scaled to the deviation. Safe: partial; no stated envelope, rate limits or confidence gate on the action. Recommendations: absent; the system acts, it does not propose and wait. Explainable: absent; no attribution, counterfactual or process-variable reasoning. Human-in-the-loop: minimal; manual termination only. The brief says these gaps are the research content of 4.1.3. Without the gate, 5 of these 10 fixed recipe commands would break the step limit or process window we later define.

Continuity, concretely: xqi keeps the seniors' structure (detector, multi-frame confirmation, critical-class stop with M112, G-code over OctoPrint) and replaces the fixed-recipe step with cause model → conformal set → counterfactual proposal → four-layer gate → human decision. The detector is a pluggable input: their YOLO model plugs in where our pipeline starts. The Printer protocol is the hand-off boundary to the team that owns the printer connection. We also re-implemented the loop as a new tested Python package rather than editing their code, because their loop code was not in the hand-over.

Rows are approaches, not products. Detection-only monitors (Baumann & Roller 2016, Delli & Chang 2018, Paraskevoudis 2020) name the defect, not the setting. CAXTON (Nature Communications 2022) infers four parameters and corrects a running printer, 84.3% test accuracy, but the split unit is not stated, so accuracy on unseen prints is unknown; no calibrated uncertainty, explanation or independent safety layer is described. The seniors' loop bounds action only by confirmation and an emergency stop. xqi has every capability, but having the capability is not the same as accuracy: our cause model's accuracy on unseen prints is the open problem (results section). This is the implemented xqi package: Python 3.11, about 4,000 lines, 270+ unit tests; every box is a module. Four run modes: baseline (seniors' fixed recipes, no gate), gated-recipe (same recipes through the gate), advisory (a human always decides) and auto (apply only when the verdict logic allows). 04 What we built

Four rules, fixed before any experiment.

Image only

The model never sees setpoints.

One door

Only the gate reaches the printer.

Fail closed

Unknown state: do nothing, ask.

Human decides

Every decision is gated again.

(1) Image-only: a model that could read the setpoints would "explain" a defect by the very setting that caused it, which fakes accuracy. (2) Setpoint changes reach the printer only through gate(); exceptions are the critical-class stop/pause and the baseline mode. The dashboard never talks to the printer. (3) The gate is a pure function of the proposal and the printer state, so it can be property-tested; a lost connection means "do nothing and ask a human". (4) Operator commands are re-gated against the current state, so a stale or forged decision cannot bypass the gate.

Three levels are kept apart: defect detection (what is visible), process-cause attribution (which setting the image is associated with) and causal explanation (whether the setting caused the defect). We build the first two plus safety; the third needs controlled experiments on a real printer. Classes: none, or low/high for one of four parameters. Labels come from the commanded setting in CAXTON, not from an independently observed defect. Images with two or more deviations (40,284) are excluded from the nine-class task. Fine-tuned end to end, AdamW, class-weighted cross-entropy, at most 100 images per print per epoch, early stopping on calibration prints.

Split-conformal prediction with LAC scores s = 1 − p(true class): the threshold q̂ is the ⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉/n quantile of the scores on calibration prints, and the set keeps every class with 1 − p ≤ q̂. Under exchangeability it covers the truth with probability ≥ 1 − α. Leave-prints-out is a distribution shift, so we report coverage empirically: 0.914 at α = 0.10 (per fold 0.879–0.981). Honest caveat: the sets average 7.1 of 9 causes, so today they say "the model can't tell" more often than not.

Each actionable cause in the conformal set maps through a fixed table to a signed change of one variable (flow_high → lower flow). Step size: 2–5 °C for temperature, 5–10% for flow and speed, scaled by a severity score. The card states cause, probability and change as one sentence: process variables, not only pixels, as the topic brief asks. Heatmap: the paper uses EigenCAM, which is class-agnostic (the same map whatever the cause). Since the paper we added Grad-CAM for the named cause, which passes a weight-randomisation sanity check; it runs after dispatch, never on the action path. Z-offset causes are never applied automatically: they produce a manual-advice card and always escalate.

Order: L2 rejects inside the cooldown and clips the step (steps 5 °C, 5 °C, 10%, 10%; cooldowns 45, 90, 20, 20 s for nozzle, bed, flow, speed). L1 rounds half-up to the integer actually sent in G-code and rejects outside the hard box (nozzle 170–240 °C, bed 0–100 °C, flow and speed 50–150%). L3 accepts only inside the PLA window or a move closer to it. L4 (in event_verdict) needs a single non-none cause with p ≥ 0.6 and no sensor flag before anything is auto-applied. Unknown printer state means reject. Property test: 100,000 random proposals, seed 0: 35,746 dispatched, 0 violations. A software result, not proof of real-printer safety.

Events that need a human go to an SQLite store with a 90 s deadline, after which the event times out to no action. The Streamlit dashboard shows the crop, conformal set, advice, sensor flags and, per proposal, the gate's per-layer result and the counterfactual sentence, with Apply, Modify, Reject, Pause and Stop. The orchestrator re-gates every decision before dispatch. Proactive path: the cause model runs on the nozzle region every frame; probabilities are smoothed (EWMA, β = 0.8) and a warning fires after 3 consecutive evaluations above θ_on, with a 60 s cooldown. Warnings are proposed, never applied.

Splitting by image would put near-identical neighbouring frames in train and test and inflate accuracy. A diagnostic shows how much: the same probe reaches balanced accuracy 0.430 when train and test share prints, 0.211 on new prints. So folds are grouped by print; 20% of each fold's training prints are calibration prints, used for early stopping, the conformal threshold and the warning threshold; test prints are used once. The majority floor (always predict the most common training cause, z_offset_high) is the bar: K2 fires unless top-1 beats it by more than 5 points.

Label audit: 1,491 of the 1,522 single-cause z_offset_low images have z ≤ −1 mm, values the CAXTON README calls incorrectly labelled; 17.5% of single-cause frames sit inside CAXTON's own 15-frame post-change gap. Post-hoc recalibration (temperature + class prior, fitted on calibration prints only) lifts the 11-19 model from 0.209 to 0.294 top-1 against a floor of 0.254 (+4.1 pp) and cuts ECE from 0.26 to 0.05, but mostly by predicting the majority cause more often. Neither clears K2. These are after the paper's results.
06 Honest limits

What we have not shown yet.

Scope

Offline only

No real printer in the loop yet.

Data

Settings, not defects

Labels name the command, not the flaw.

Claim

Not causation

Needs controlled runs on a printer.

(1) Everything was measured offline on CAXTON and simulated printers: no physical printer, no configured detector, no end-to-end latency on hardware. (2) CAXTON has setting labels only (one camera design, PLA, eight printers); it cannot validate the four defect categories or a causal claim; multi-deviation images are excluded. (3) Even a perfect cause model only associates images with commanded settings. Also open: explanations not yet tested with people (the operator study has no valid participants), event-to-card p95 35 s misses its 1 s target, and the YOLOv8 figures of the accompanying manuscript could not be verified.
