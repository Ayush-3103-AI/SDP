One-line story: our seniors' printer already detects defects and fixes them with fixed recipes. We add the missing half: which setting is likely off, how sure the model is, what to change, and a safety gate that bounds any change before a human approves it. Steps 02 and 03 (highlighted) are this project's core contribution. xqi = explainable quality intelligence, CARR topic 4.1.3. Everything measured is offline on the public CAXTON data and a simulated printer.
01 The problem

A failing print is visible. Its cause is not.

Each defect can come from several settings, so seeing the defect doesn't tell you what to change.

FFF is the most common desktop additive process and one of the least forgiving: a bad flow rate, speed, nozzle temperature or nozzle height ruins a print within a few layers. The map shows why a defect name alone is not actionable: under-extrusion alone can come from low flow, high speed, a cold nozzle or a nozzle set too high. Layer shift is mostly mechanical; stringing is mostly temperature (our prior also lists low speed). A camera sees the defect; it cannot directly see the setting.

Detection is a mature field: it answers "what". The operator's next three questions are open: why the print is failing, what to change, and whether that change is safe to make while the printer runs. A system that changes printer settings on its own from a model that may be wrong adds a failure mode of its own, so safety has to be answered independently of model quality. This project addresses the three orange questions.

The brief's own audit: quality intelligence present but severity is binary; corrective present but recipes fixed; safe partial (no envelope, rate limits or confidence gate on the action); recommendations absent; explainable absent; human-in-the-loop minimal. Rows are approaches, not products. Detection-only monitors name the defect, not the setting. CAXTON infers four parameters and corrects a running printer (84.3% test accuracy) but the split unit is not stated, so accuracy on unseen prints is unknown; no calibrated uncertainty, explanation or independent safety layer is described. 04 What we built

Four rules, fixed before any experiment.

1

Image only

The model never sees setpoints, so it can't "explain" by reading them.

2

One door

Only the gate can change a printer setting.

3

Fail closed

Unknown printer state means do nothing and ask.

4

Human decides

Every operator click is checked by the gate again.

(1) A model that could read the setpoints would "explain" a defect by the very setting that caused it, which fakes accuracy. (2) Setpoint changes reach the printer only through gate(); exceptions are the critical-class stop/pause and the baseline mode. The dashboard never talks to the printer. (3) The gate is a pure function of the proposal and printer state, so it can be property-tested; a lost connection means "do nothing and ask a human". (4) Operator commands are re-gated against the current state, so a stale or forged decision cannot bypass the gate.

The real demo crop shown here (a CAXTON flow-high frame) gets "flow too high" at p = 0.38. Classes: none, or low/high for exactly one of four parameters. Labels come from the commanded setting in CAXTON, not from an independently observed defect, so a "correct" prediction means agreement with that label, not discovery of a root cause. Three levels are kept apart: defect detection, process-cause attribution (this model) and causal explanation (needs controlled experiments on a real printer). Images with two or more deviations (40,284) are excluded from this nine-class task.

Split-conformal prediction with LAC scores s = 1 − p(true class): the cut-off q̂ is the ⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉/n quantile of the scores on calibration prints (α = 0.10), and the set keeps every class with 1 − p ≤ q̂. Each actionable cause in the set maps through a fixed table to a signed change of one variable (flow high → lower flow). Step size: 2–5 °C for temperature, 5–10% for flow and speed, scaled by a severity score. The sentence is in process variables, not only pixels, as the topic brief asks. Heatmap: the paper uses EigenCAM, which is class-agnostic. Since the paper we added Grad-CAM for the named cause (shown, real model output), which passes a weight-randomisation sanity check; it runs after dispatch, never on the action path. It shows where the network looked, not that the setting caused the defect.

Order: L2 rejects inside the cooldown and clips the step (steps 5 °C, 5 °C, 10%, 10%; cooldowns 45, 90, 20, 20 s for nozzle, bed, flow, speed). L1 rounds half-up to the integer actually sent in G-code and rejects outside the hard box (nozzle 170–240 °C, bed 0–100 °C, flow and speed 50–150%). L3 accepts only inside the PLA window or a move closer to it. L4 (event_verdict) needs a single non-none cause with p ≥ 0.6 and no sensor flag before anything is auto-applied. Unknown printer state means reject. Property test: 100,000 random proposals, seed 0: 35,746 dispatched, 0 violations. A software result, not proof of real-printer safety.

Events that need a human go to an SQLite store with a 90 s deadline; after it the event times out to no action. The Streamlit dashboard shows the crop, conformal set, advice, sensor flags and, per proposal, the gate's per-layer result and the counterfactual sentence. The orchestrator re-gates every decision before dispatch. Proactive path (not drawn): the cause model also runs on the nozzle region every frame; probabilities are smoothed (EWMA, β = 0.8) and an early warning fires after 3 consecutive evaluations above a threshold, with a 60 s cooldown. CAXTON: Brion & Pattinson, Nature Communications 2022 · CC BY 4.0.

Splitting by image puts near-identical neighbouring frames in train and test and inflates accuracy: the same frozen probe reaches balanced accuracy 0.430 when train and test share prints, but 0.211 on new prints. So folds are grouped by print; 20% of each fold's training prints are calibration prints, used for early stopping, the conformal cut-off and the warning threshold; test prints are used once. The bar is the majority floor (always predict the most common training cause, z_offset_high): kill criterion K2 fires unless top-1 beats it by more than 5 points.

06 Honest limits

What we have not shown yet.

Scope

Offline only

Data replay and a simulated printer; no real printer in the loop.

NEEDSThe seniors' rig, gate in the loop.
Data

Settings, not defects

Labels record the command, not the visible flaw.

NEEDSDefect-annotated data from our printers.
Claim

Not causation

The model links images to settings; that is not cause and effect.

NEEDSControlled interventions per setting.
(1) Everything was measured offline on CAXTON and simulated printers: no physical printer, no configured detector, no end-to-end latency on hardware. (2) CAXTON has setting labels only (one camera design, PLA, eight printers); it cannot validate the four defect categories or a causal claim; multi-deviation images are excluded. (3) Even a perfect cause model only associates images with commanded settings. Also open: explanations not yet tested with people (no valid operator-study participants yet), event-to-card p95 of 35 s misses its 1 s target, and the YOLOv8 figures of the accompanying manuscript could not be verified.
